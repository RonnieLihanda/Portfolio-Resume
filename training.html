Training people to use AI and automation tools

Ajira Digital (Mastercard Foundation) and the Norwegian Refugee Council. Kenya.

What I do

I teach non-technical people to use AI and automation tools well enough to work with them the same day. The goal of every session is a working result the learner built themselves, not a slide deck they will forget.

Young people in Kenya

  • More than 2,000 people trained on AI and automation tools through the Ajira Digital programme, funded by the Mastercard Foundation and delivered through eMobilis.
  • Tools taught: ChatGPT, Zapier, n8n, Airtable and Slack, framed around remote work and freelancing.
  • Practical curriculum. Each module is built around a real workflow, so participants leave with a working automation and not only the theory.
  • Reach. Physical trainings across the South Rift region, with a focus on bringing young women into digital work.

Refugee and humanitarian settings

As Operations and Training Coordinator with the Norwegian Refugee Council in Dadaab, I coordinated work between field teams and headquarters, kept the operating procedures current and produced structured reports for international stakeholders. Training in this setting means clear materials, few assumptions about connectivity or prior experience, and records that the next person can follow.

How a session runs

  1. Start from a job the learner already does, such as tracking clients, answering enquiries or sending a weekly update.
  2. Show the manual version, then build the automated one live.
  3. Have each participant rebuild it on their own data.
  4. Leave behind a one-page guide so the skill survives after the room empties.

Available for

Corporate team enablement on AI tools, cohort training for youth and women's programmes, and workshops for NGOs and donor-funded projects.
